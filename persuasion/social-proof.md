# Social Proof

## 1. What is it?

Social proof is a persuasion principle based on the idea that people often look at the actions and experiences of others when deciding what to do.

In marketing and communication, social proof can show that other people have used, recommended, or interacted with a product, service, or organization. Examples include customer reviews, ratings, expert recommendations, user numbers, and community feedback.

## 2. How do you recognize or use it?

Social proof can be recognized when a message includes real evidence of what other people have done or experienced.

For example, a website might show verified customer reviews, ratings, recommendations from qualified experts, or feedback from a community. This information can help an audience feel more confident when considering an option.

Social proof should be truthful and clearly presented. Organizations should not create fake reviews, testimonials, or statistics.

## 3. Example

### Verified Customer Reviews

Imagine an online bookstore displaying verified reviews from customers who purchased a book. A potential customer can read these reviews to learn about other customers' experiences before deciding whether to buy the book.

This uses social proof because the experiences of other customers provide information that may influence a person's decision.

**Persuasion principle:** Social Proof

**Application:** Use genuine customer reviews or other verifiable feedback to help an audience evaluate an option.
