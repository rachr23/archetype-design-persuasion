# Memphis Design

## 1. What is it?

Memphis Design is a postmodernist design movement that began in Milan, Italy, in 1981. It was created by a group of designers led by Ettore Sottsass. The movement challenged the simple and functional approach of modernist design by using playful shapes, bright colors, patterns, and unusual combinations.

Memphis Design is known for its bold and expressive appearance. It often combines geometric shapes, squiggly lines, dots, stripes, and contrasting colors.

## 2. How do you recognize or use it?

Memphis Design can be recognized by bright colors, geometric shapes, abstract patterns, irregular lines, and playful compositions. Designers often combine shapes and patterns in unexpected ways.

The style can be used for posters, websites, advertisements, packaging, furniture, branding, and other visual designs. It can make a design feel energetic, creative, and playful.

## 3. Examples

### Carlton Room Divider — Ettore Sottsass

The Carlton Room Divider was designed by Ettore Sottsass in 1981 and is one of the best-known objects associated with the Memphis Group. It uses colorful geometric shapes and an unusual structure instead of traditional furniture design.

**Creator:** Ettore Sottsass  
**Title:** Carlton Room Divider  
**Date:** 1981  
**Movement:** Memphis Group

[Source: Museum of Modern Art — Carlton](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/82191)

### Memphis Group Furniture and Objects

The Memphis Group created furniture and household objects that used bold colors, geometric forms, patterns, and unconventional combinations. These designs challenged traditional ideas about what furniture should look like.

**Design Group:** Memphis Group  
**Period:** 1980s  
**Location:** Milan, Italy

[Source: Design Museum — Memphis Group](https://designmuseum.org/discover-design/all-stories/memphis-group-awful-or-awesome)
